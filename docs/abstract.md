# Abstract (draft)

## Abstract

Knowing where road collisions are likely to concentrate next month helps agencies target inspection and enforcement. We forecast, for each 500 m British National Grid cell, whether the next month will contain at least two police-recorded collisions, using 513,748 UK collisions from 2021 to 2025. Features describe each cell's own collision history and use only months up to the prediction month; a synthetic-grid test checks this. We compare logistic regression, random forest and histogram gradient boosting under a strict chronological protocol: training on targets to 2023, tuning, calibration and threshold selection on 2024, and one evaluation on 2025 with the configuration frozen and hashed beforehand. The selected model ranks cells better than a trailing-12-month collision count, but only modestly, and the three learning algorithms are practically indistinguishable. DBSCAN clusters, fitted on training years only, were tested as additional features and did not help (random-forest ablation, validation average precision 0.2480 with clusters versus 0.2510 without), so the final model uses none. Because only cells with at least three collisions in the training years are modelled, at most 91.7% of 2025 hotspot cell-months can be reached, which caps recall. Predictions are delivered as rank-based monthly risk tiers in a Streamlit dashboard. Results are limited by a single test year, cell-level uncertainty intervals that ignore spatial autocorrelation, and a model that does not use the most recent full year for fitting.

## Results

On the 2025 test set (364,656 cell-months, 8,549 hotspots, base rate 2.34%), the frozen histogram gradient boosting model reached average precision 0.255 (10.9 times the base rate) and ROC-AUC 0.862. Ranking cells by trailing-12-month collisions gave average precision 0.220, and last-month persistence gave 0.075. The gain over the trailing-12-month baseline was +0.035 average precision (95% grid-bootstrap CI +0.031 to +0.038) and +0.014 precision in the top 5% of cells per month (CI +0.010 to +0.019); the top-1% precision difference (+0.016, CI -0.0002 to +0.030) was not distinguishable from zero. Precision in the top 1% of cells each month was 0.437 with recall 0.187. At the frozen threshold, precision was 0.289, recall 0.360 and F1 0.320. Calibrated probabilities had a Brier score of 0.0198 versus 0.0229 for the constant base-rate forecast. On 2024 validation the three model families differed by at most 0.0020 average precision, and DBSCAN cluster features lowered it by 0.0030. Modelled cells contained 91.7% of 2025 hotspot cell-months, so system-wide recall at the threshold was 0.330.

## Limitations

Bootstrap intervals resample grid cells only, not years or model seeds, and treat neighbouring cells as independent although they are spatially correlated. The final model was trained on targets to 2023 only, with 2024 used for calibration and the threshold. Results come from one test year. The test-set intervals were computed after the evaluation, for reporting only.
